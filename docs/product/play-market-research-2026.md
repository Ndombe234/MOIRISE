# MORISE PLAY — Market Research & Design Direction (September 2026)

## Executive reading

Roblox's June 2026 discovery update shifted recommendation toward longer-term retention, repeat play and co-play rather than short-lived clicks. Its discovery documentation also describes personalized retrieval/ranking using engagement, retention and social signals. Source: https://about.roblox.com/en-nz/newsroom/2026/06/optimizing-discovery-great-games-reach-millions-players-roblox

UGC platforms continue to expand the role of the player as creator. BCG describes UGC as a major creator-economy force, while Naavik estimates creator payouts across Roblox, Fortnite and Overwolf reached about $2.2B in 2025. Sources: https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/video-gaming-report-2026-next-era-of-growth and https://naavik.co/deep-dives/the-state-of-ugc-games-2026/

GameAnalytics reports that retention pressure remains high in 2026: most games still see short, infrequent sessions while top performers capture a disproportionate share of long-term engagement. Source: https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-mobile-pc-gaming-benchmarks

## What this means for MORISE

### 1. One PLAY button is stronger than a catalogue wall

Keep the primary loop simple: PLAY -> one recommended experience -> result -> next possibility.

### 2. Optimize for long-term value, not click bait

Measure first-play completion, replay, repeat-day return, meaningful session quality, solo continuation, social continuation, creation/discovery output and shareable Moments.

### 3. Unique means mechanically distinct

Changing art while keeping the same interaction does not count as a new MORISE game. A useful internal formula is: new game = core verb + MORISE twist + meaningful consequence + shareable moment.

### 4. Short games need a second layer

A 30–90 second experience should be able to reveal a new route, variant, artifact, rule, challenge seed or SYSTEM signal on later attempts.

### 5. Creation should be embedded in the world

Use small acts of creation—routes, rules, patterns, scenes, hidden paths, challenge seeds—as objects that can reappear elsewhere in MORISE.

### 6. Sharing should be evidence-based

Natural share reasons are discovery, personal bests, artifacts, unusual combinations and challenges. Do not pay users to spam invitations.

## Candidate concept pool — NOT the canonical 40-game inventory

These are design candidates only and must not replace the authoritative inventory.

- Inverse Memory — remember a pattern and the transformation rule that reverses it.
- Quiet Interval — win by acting during a counterintuitive silent window.
- Split Second — detect one changing detail and carry that decision into the next scene.
- Echo Door — a tiny world where doors appear only after actions performed elsewhere.
- Gravity Thread — connect nodes while gravity changes after each connection.
- False Shortcut — a tempting shortcut works once; discovering why becomes the puzzle.
- Rulemaker — invent a tiny rule; MORISE generates a legal challenge from it.
- Signal Garden — create a visual pattern that becomes another player's timing challenge.
- Pocket World — place five objects; their arrangement becomes a micro-world with a hidden interaction.
- Shadow Relay — compete against another player's recorded route asynchronously.
- One-Turn Trap — each player changes the board once before handing it off.
- Lost Fragment — multiple small experiences reveal pieces of one hidden signal.
- Crossroads — the next activity reacts to the kind of mistake just made.
- Folded City — a small 3D world where perspective reconnects distant spaces.

## Rules for every new game

1. What is the core verb?
2. What makes it recognizably MORISE?
3. What changes after success or failure?
4. What is different on a second attempt?
5. What can become a Moment?
6. What does it contribute to SYSTEM?
7. Is the solo path complete?
8. Does the mechanic justify 2D or 3D?
9. Why would someone share it without being paid to?
10. What evidence would justify more exposure?
