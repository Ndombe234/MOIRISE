# MORISE — Fun, Games & Sharing Direction

## Product decision

MORISE should not become a directory of games. It should become a world where **play, discovery, creation and sharing feed one another**.

A Player can enter alone, have fun immediately, and naturally produce something worth showing another person.

## Market signals used

- In 2026, discovery increasingly happens through short-form/video and creator surfaces; Meta reports Reels as a major discovery surface in India, with 89% of surveyed Gen Z users engaging daily. This supports visual, shareable moments, but MORISE should avoid becoming a simple short-video clone.
- Current gaming growth is increasingly tied to retention layers, tasks, co-op, live events and player creation rather than endless one-off installs.
- UGC remains a major engagement model: research on Roblox/Fortnite/Overwolf shows substantial creator payouts and continued growth of user-created experiences.
- Unity's 2026 report highlights online events, social media, multiplayer, cross-play, daily missions and leaderboards as common engagement/discoverability investments.
- Casual gaming is mature: copying a saturated genre is less interesting than combining a very simple core loop with a distinctive meta-layer.

## MORISE opportunity

### 1. Shareable Moments

Every meaningful action can optionally produce a compact **Moment Card**:

- what the Player did
- achievement or discovery
- visual identity from the SYSTEM
- short title
- timestamp/event context
- share button
- privacy controls

The card should be useful even when shared outside MORISE. It must never expose private data by default.

### 2. Solo → Social bridge

A Player does not need friends to start.

Examples:

- finish a solo challenge → publish a challenge result
- discover an unusual topic → send the discovery to another Player
- create a mini-game → invite others asynchronously
- complete a collection → share the collection card
- beat a personal record → challenge a community

This is the core loop:

**PLAY → CREATE A MOMENT → SHARE → DISCOVER OTHERS → CONNECT → PLAY AGAIN**

### 3. Micro-games first

Before attempting large 3D games, MORISE should support a library of small games that load quickly and work well on mobile/web:

- reflex
- puzzle
- memory
- strategy
- word/language
- logic
- rhythm
- exploration toys
- asynchronous challenges

Each game should have a clear one-sentence rule and a short first session.

### 4. 2D and 3D are both allowed

3D should be used when it materially improves the experience. It should not be added merely because it looks impressive.

2D should be preferred for games where speed, clarity and accessibility are more important.

Future 3D experiences can include:

- small explorable rooms
- social hubs
- obstacle courses
- collection spaces
- creator-made mini-worlds

### 5. Player-made experiences

The long-term platform should allow Players to create lightweight activities and games without requiring professional game-development skills.

A creator could start from:

**Idea → template → rules → appearance → publish → play → share → improve**

This is more aligned with MORISE than simply embedding a third-party game catalogue.

### 6. Surprise without chaos

The Discovery engine should occasionally surface a deliberate **Detour**:

> You normally explore X. MORISE found Y because of an action you just took.

Detours should be understandable and dismissible. They should not permanently classify the Player.

### 7. Social loops without requiring simultaneous users

To preserve the solo-first principle, many experiences should support:

- asynchronous challenges
- cohort leaderboards
- shared milestones
- community goals
- rematches
- reaction cards
- collaborative collections

A dead friend list must never make MORISE feel empty.

## What we should NOT copy

Do not clone:

- TikTok's feed
- Roblox's full platform model
- Fortnite's creative stack
- Discord's server model
- Solo Leveling's exact UI/art
- conventional battle-pass spam
- infinite notification badges
- artificial streak pressure

MORISE should combine useful patterns while keeping its own identity.

## Sharing principle

Sharing must feel like **showing something you did**, not advertising MORISE.

Good:

> "I just discovered this."  
> "I beat my record."  
> "Look what I created."  
> "Try this challenge."

Avoid:

> "Come back every day or lose your streak."  
> "Invite 10 friends to unlock this."  
> "You are missing 7 notifications."

## Proposed future module order

1. World/Home foundation
2. Discovery/Curiosity Map
3. Micro-games engine
4. Moment Cards + sharing
5. Activities/challenges
6. Communities
7. Events
8. Creator tools
9. Player-created games/experiences
10. Larger 3D experiences when justified by usage

The order is deliberately designed to validate fun and sharing before investing heavily in complex 3D infrastructure.
