# MOIRISE — Game Market Research Protocol

## Purpose

Every new MOIRISE game must be validated as a product before significant implementation work begins.

## Required research

### 1. Demand
Search current evidence for the requested genre/subgenre:
- search interest where reliable;
- active communities;
- current releases;
- player discussions;
- platform activity;
- observable engagement signals.

### 2. Competition
Identify relevant comparable games and document:
- core loop;
- session length;
- solo/collective modes;
- progression;
- social systems;
- monetization model;
- common complaints;
- differentiating mechanics.

Do not copy protected characters, art, worlds, branded rules, or other protected expression.

### 3. Player needs
Use reviews/community discussion to identify:
- what players praise;
- what frustrates them;
- missing features;
- complexity barriers;
- onboarding problems;
- retention reasons.

### 4. MOIRISE opportunity
For each candidate, answer:

- Why would a MOIRISE player try it?
- Why would they play again?
- Why would they share it?
- Why would they play solo?
- Why would they invite others?
- What can MOIRISE do differently?

### 5. Technical feasibility
Estimate:
- frontend complexity;
- backend complexity;
- real-time requirement;
- database needs;
- anti-cheat requirements;
- mobile performance;
- hosting cost/scale risk;
- development time.

## Decision framework

Do not produce a single arbitrary “best game” score. Present the evidence and trade-offs across:

- demand evidence;
- differentiation;
- feasibility;
- replayability;
- social potential;
- solo quality;
- collective quality;
- development risk.

The product owner makes the final choice.

## Prototype gate

Before full implementation:

1. define the core loop;
2. build the smallest playable version;
3. test the first session;
4. test solo;
5. test collective mode when relevant;
6. measure completion/confusion/failure points;
7. revise rules/UI;
8. only then expand.

## No invented market data

If exact demand data is unavailable, say so. Use qualitative signals rather than fabricated percentages or player counts.

## Research record

For every selected game, create a short research record containing:

- date;
- question;
- sources;
- findings;
- assumptions;
- risks;
- decision;
- next experiment.
